## 1. Business context

A real printing and publishing company in Palestine, Ibn Khaldoun Printing and Publishing, gave us a real production problem.

In a cutting job, the factory may issue a large amount of board/paper material, but management needs to know exactly where that material went and distinguish true waste from reusable leftover pieces.

Example:

Job IK-202 on cutting machine CT-2:

* 1,200 kg board issued
* 900 kg good cut product
* 180 kg setup/layout scrap
* 60 kg damaged scrap
* 60 kg reusable offcuts

Therefore:

* Total scrap = 240 kg = 20%
* Good yield = 900 / 1,200 = 75%
* Reusable offcuts = 60 kg = 5%
* Total = 100%

Comparison job IK-203 on the same machine:

* 1,000 kg issued
* 820 kg good product
* 100 kg scrap
* 80 kg reusable offcuts
* Scrap rate = 10%

So IK-202 has a scrap rate 10 percentage points higher than IK-203.

The largest scrap category in IK-202 is setup/layout scrap at 180 kg.

The original challenge asks for a digital job card where the operator records:

* material
* machine
* issued material
* good output
* scrap by reason
* reusable offcuts
* rework separately if relevant

The application should validate that the quantities balance back to the issued material, calculate yield/scrap, and allow comparison between jobs.

## 2. Important product-thinking requirement

Do NOT design this as a one-off application only for Ibn Khaldoun.

The Ibn Khaldoun case is our pilot use case.

We want to turn the underlying problem into a marketable product for other material-intensive manufacturing businesses such as:

* carton producers
* commercial printers
* label producers
* packaging/converting companies
* paper converters
* other factories where material enters a production job and leaves as good output, scrap, reusable leftovers, rework, or other categorized outcomes

The product concept is:

### Material Loss Intelligence

Core promise:
"Make every production job explain where its material went, distinguish true scrap from reusable material, and help managers identify unusual or avoidable material losses."

This should NOT try to become a full ERP.
It should NOT try to replace a cutting-optimization engine.
It should NOT depend on complex machine integrations for the MVP.

The product should focus on job-level material visibility, validation, comparison, and actionable loss insights.

## 3. Product direction

Think in this flow:

Material In
→ Production Job
→ Good Output
→ Scrap by reason
→ Reusable Offcuts
→ Rework / Other
→ Balance validation
→ Yield + Scrap metrics
→ Comparison / baseline
→ Loss insights
→ Investigation support

The core value is not just recording numbers.
The value is helping a supervisor or manager understand:

* Where did the material go?
* What is the true scrap?
* Which loss category is largest?
* Is this job abnormal compared with comparable jobs?
* What should be investigated?

## 4. AI role

AI should be an assistant layer, not the foundation of the calculation.

The deterministic application must calculate:

* totals
* balance
* yield
* scrap rate
* reusable percentage
* comparisons

AI may then summarize the evidence in plain language.

Example acceptable AI output:
"IK-202 recorded 20% scrap compared with 10% for IK-203 on the same machine. Setup/layout scrap accounts for 180 kg of the 240 kg total scrap and is the largest recorded loss category. Further investigation is recommended."

AI must NOT invent a root cause.

For example, it must NOT say:
"The machine was incorrectly configured"
unless the user explicitly entered evidence supporting that claim.

AI should only interpret recorded data and operator/supervisor notes.

## 6. MVP requirements

The first version should be intentionally focused and realistically buildable quickly.

Core features expected:

### A. Dashboard

Show:

* total jobs
* total material issued
* total good output
* total true scrap
* total reusable offcuts
* average yield
* average scrap rate
* recent / notable jobs
* a simple indication of abnormal or high-loss jobs

### B. Create / close a production job

Fields should support:

* Job ID
* Product
* Material
* Machine
* Issued material weight
* Good output weight
* Scrap by reason
* Reusable offcuts
* Rework if included in the model
* Operator/supervisor notes if useful

Do not force unnecessary fields.

### C. Balance validation

The system must verify:

Issued Material =
Good Output

* Scrap
* Reusable Offcuts
* Rework / Other (only if included)

A job must not be considered valid/closed if the numbers do not balance within a clearly defined tolerance.

The UI should explain the imbalance clearly instead of just showing "error".

### D. Job analysis

For each job show:

* issued
* good output
* true scrap
* reusable
* yield %
* scrap %
* breakdown of scrap reasons
* largest loss category
* any comparison result available

### E. Comparison

The system should compare a job against relevant previous jobs.

At minimum, support comparison by:

* same machine
* and ideally same/similar material or product when enough data exists

If there is insufficient historical data, the UI should say so rather than creating a misleading comparison.

### F. Investigation insight

For jobs with unusually high scrap, surface:

* the main loss category
* how much it contributed
* comparison to baseline/comparable jobs
* a suggested investigation area

This should be framed as investigation guidance, NOT diagnosis.

### G. AI summary

A simple button such as "Analyze Job" or "Generate Insight" can produce a concise evidence-based summary.

Do not overbuild AI.

The product should feel like a real modern B2B manufacturing product, not a school project.
